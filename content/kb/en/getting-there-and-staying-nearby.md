Treatment can mean many trips to the hospital, sometimes far from home. Rides, parking and a place to sleep add up quickly. Ask for help early. You don't have to have a crisis first.

## Start with the social worker

The hospital social worker usually knows what help is available for families, both at the hospital and nearby. Ask:

- Is there help with gas, parking, public transportation or rides?
- Is there family housing, or a place to stay near the hospital at a lower cost?
- Are there meal programs or kitchens for families?

## Parking

- Ask at the front desk whether the hospital offers parking discounts or passes for families with frequent visits.
- Keep your parking receipts. Some help programs ask for them.

## Rides

- Some insurance plans cover rides to medical appointments. Call the member services number on your insurance card and ask.
- Ask the social worker about other ride options in your area.
- Family, friends and neighbors often want to help. Driving to an appointment is a clear, useful way to do it.

## Staying nearby

- If you live far away, ask about lodging before the first long stay, if you can.
- Ask what the place offers: kitchen, laundry, shuttle to the hospital, and whether siblings can stay too.
- Ask how long you can stay and whether there is a waitlist.

## Make long days easier

- Keep a bag ready in the car or by the door: snacks, water, chargers, a change of clothes, something for your child to do.
- Leave extra time for traffic and parking.
- Save the hospital address and the unit's phone in your phone.
- If you're driving after a long or hard day and feel too tired, it's okay to ask someone else to drive.

## Who to ask

The hospital social worker for transportation, lodging and meal help. Your insurance plan to ask whether rides are covered. The front desk about parking.
