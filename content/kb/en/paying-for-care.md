Bills and insurance letters can pile up quickly. You don't have to figure them out alone. Hospitals have people whose job is to help families with exactly this.

## Start with two people

- **The hospital social worker** can help you find help with costs like travel, meals, lodging and other daily needs.
- **A financial counselor** (sometimes called a patient financial advocate) can help with hospital bills, payment plans and financial assistance. Ask the front desk or the social worker how to reach one.

## Questions for your insurance plan

Call the member services number on your insurance card. Write down the date, the name of the person you spoke with and what they said.

- Is this hospital and this care team in our network?
- Does any treatment, test or stay need approval before it happens?
- Is there a case manager who can help us with our child's care?
- What will we have to pay, and is there a yearly limit on what we pay?
- Are travel or lodging costs ever covered?

## Keep your papers together

- Keep bills, insurance letters and receipts in one folder.
- Insurance plans often send a letter that explains what they paid. It's usually not a bill. Compare it with the hospital's bill before you pay.
- Keep receipts for travel, parking and meals. Some help programs ask for them.

## Before you pay a bill

- If you don't understand a bill, ask the financial counselor to go through it with you.
- Ask if there are mistakes, if insurance still needs to pay part of it, or if you can get financial assistance.
- Ask about a payment plan if you can't pay all at once.

## Ask about help programs

Many hospitals have financial assistance programs for families who qualify. The social worker can also tell you about other kinds of help that may fit your family. Ask, even if you're not sure you'd qualify.

## Who to ask

The hospital social worker and financial counselor, first. Your insurance plan, for questions about coverage.
