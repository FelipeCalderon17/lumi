Many parents have to work out how to keep a job while caring for a child in treatment. There's no single right answer, and plans often change as treatment goes on. A few early conversations can make things easier.

## Talk to your employer's HR team

Human resources (HR) can explain the options that apply to you. Ask:

- What kinds of leave can I take, paid or unpaid?
- Can I take leave a few hours or days at a time, not only all at once?
- Can I change my schedule or work from home on some days?
- What forms do I need, and who has to fill them in?
- Will my health insurance continue while I'm on leave?

Some workers have legal protections when they need time off to care for a family member. What applies to you depends on your job and where you live, so ask HR, and get the answers in writing.

## Paperwork from the hospital

Leave forms sometimes need to be filled in by your child's doctor. Give the forms to the care team early, because they can take time. The hospital social worker can help you know what to ask for.

## What to tell your manager and coworkers

You decide how much to share. It can be enough to say: "My child is having medical treatment, and I may need time off at short notice." You don't have to share details.

## Share the load

- If there are two caregivers, think about taking turns at the hospital, so one person can keep working or rest.
- Make a list of people who could cover school pickups, meals or hospital shifts.
- Write down the plan for the week so everyone knows who is doing what.

## If you lose income

Talk to the hospital social worker early. They can help you look for financial help and other support. See "Paying for care: getting financial help".

## Look after yourself too

Working and caregiving at the same time is very tiring. Be honest with yourself about what you can do this week, and ask for help with the rest.

## Who to ask

Your employer's HR team for leave and schedule options. The hospital social worker for forms, letters and financial help.
