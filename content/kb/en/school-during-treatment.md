School is more than lessons. It's friends, routine and a sense of being a regular kid. During treatment, your child may miss days or weeks, so a plan with the school helps them keep up and stay connected.

## Talk with the school early

- Meet with your child's teacher, and if you can, the school counselor or school nurse.
- Share what you're comfortable sharing. You decide how much the class is told.
- Agree on one person at school who will be your main contact.
- Ask how they'll send schoolwork, and how much is realistic right now.

## Ask about support plans

Schools can often set up a formal plan for students with health needs, like extra time, a lighter workload or work sent home. Ask the school counselor what's available and how to request it. Get what you agree on in writing.

## Learning at the hospital

Some hospitals have teachers or school programs for children in treatment. Ask the care team or the social worker if yours does, and how they work with your child's school.

## Staying connected with friends

- Classmates can send cards, drawings or videos.
- Video calls with a friend or the class can help, if your child wants them.
- Older kids may want to choose what friends are told, and when.

## Going back to school

- Ask your child's care team when it's okay to go back, and whether there are any rules, like avoiding crowds or certain activities.
- Share those rules with the school in writing.
- Talk with your child about what friends might ask, and practice simple answers together.
- Going back part-time at first is often easier.

## Brothers and sisters

Let your other children's schools know too, so teachers can understand changes in mood or homework. See "Talking with your other children".

## Who to ask

Your child's teacher and school counselor for plans and schoolwork. The care team for when your child can go back and what to avoid. The hospital social worker for help between the two.
