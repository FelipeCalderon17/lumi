The first days often come fast, and you may pack in a hurry. You don't need everything at once. Bring the basics, and let family or friends bring the rest later.

## Papers

- Your ID and your child's insurance card.
- Any forms or letters the hospital sent you.
- A notebook and pen, or a notes app, for names, times and questions. See "Keeping track: a family notebook".

## For your child

- Comfortable clothes that are easy to change, and socks or slippers.
- Something from home that feels familiar: a blanket, a pillowcase, a favorite stuffed animal.
- Things to do: books, coloring, a tablet with headphones, a small game.
- For older kids and teens: their phone, charger and anything that helps them feel like themselves.

Each unit has its own rules about food, flowers, toys and things from home. Ask a nurse what is allowed before you bring something special.

## For you

- A change of clothes, a sweater and comfortable shoes. Hospital rooms can be cold.
- Toothbrush, toiletries and anything you use every day.
- Your own medicines, if you take any.
- A phone charger with a long cable. Outlets are often far from the chair.
- A water bottle and snacks. Meals can be easy to miss.
- Some cash or a card for parking, vending machines or the cafeteria.

## Leave at home

- Jewelry, large amounts of cash and anything you'd hate to lose.

## Questions for your first day

- Where can parents sleep, shower and store food?
- Where is the cafeteria, and when is it open?
- Who is our nurse today, and how do we reach them?
- Who can help with parking, meals or a place to stay nearby? Often this is the hospital social worker.

## Who to ask

Your child's nurse knows the unit's rules. The hospital social worker can help with practical things like parking, meals and lodging.
