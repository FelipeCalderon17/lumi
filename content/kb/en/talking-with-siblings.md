Brothers and sisters often feel worried, left out or even jealous, and then guilty for feeling that way. Simple, honest updates and a little time of their own help a lot.

## Tell them what's going on

- Use simple, honest words that fit their age. Children often imagine something worse than the truth when no one explains.
- Tell them it isn't anyone's fault. Siblings sometimes also worry they could get sick too. Ask the care team how to answer that. They hear the question often.
- Let them ask questions, even the same ones many times.
- Give short updates, especially when plans change.

## Keep some things the same

- Keep their routines where you can: school, activities, bedtime.
- Ask a relative, neighbor or friend to help with rides, meals or bedtime on hospital days.
- Tell them who will pick them up and when. Not knowing is hard for children.

## Give them time of their own

- Even ten minutes alone with you, doing something they choose, matters.
- A call, a message or a voice note from the hospital helps them feel remembered.
- Notice the things they do well, not only the help they give.

## Help them feel included

- Let them send drawings, cards or videos to their brother or sister.
- Ask the care team whether siblings can visit, and what the rules are on the unit.
- Some hospitals have programs or support groups for siblings. The social worker will know.

## Tell the school

A short note to their teacher or school counselor helps. Then the school can understand changes in mood, sleep or homework, and offer support.

## When they may need more help

If you notice changes that last, like trouble sleeping, pulling away from friends, or falling behind at school, talk with the school counselor or the hospital social worker. Asking for help is part of caring for them.

## Who to ask

The hospital social worker or a child life specialist for ideas and sibling programs. Their teacher or school counselor for support at school.
