Your child's care team will tell you which signs mean you should call them right away. Those signs are different for every child and every treatment, so only your care team can give you that list. Lumi can't, and won't guess.

## Get your list

- Ask: "What signs mean we should call you right away? Can we have that in writing?"
- Ask: "What number do we call during the day, and what number at night and on weekends?"
- Ask: "When should we go to the emergency room, and which one?"
- If something on the list is unclear, ask them to explain it in different words.

## Keep it where you can see it

- Put the list on the fridge or next to your child's bed.
- Take a photo of it on your phone.
- Save the care team's numbers in your phone and write them in your notebook.
- Make sure anyone who cares for your child has the list and the numbers too: a partner, grandparents, a babysitter.

## When in doubt, call

You know your child best. If something feels wrong, even if it isn't on the list, call your care team. Care teams would much rather hear from you early than late. You are not bothering them.

## In an emergency, call 911

If you think it's an emergency, call 911 right away. Outside the US, contact your local emergency services.

## What to have ready when you call

- Your child's name and date of birth.
- What you're noticing, and when it started.
- Your notebook, with the list of medicines your care team gave you.
- A pen to write down what they tell you.

## After the call

Write down what they said and what to do next. If they asked you to call back or come in, write down when.

## Who to ask

Your child's care team, for the list of urgent signs and the numbers to call. In an emergency, 911.
