During treatment there are many names, dates, papers and instructions. A family notebook keeps them in one place, so you don't have to hold everything in your head. It can be a binder, a notebook or a notes app. Use whatever you'll actually keep up.

## What to put in it

**Contacts**

- Your child's care team, with the numbers to call during the day and after hours.
- The hospital social worker, the school, your employer's HR team and your insurance plan.

**Calendar**

- Appointments, treatments and tests, with times and places.
- School days missed and work days off, if you need to track them.

**Medicines list from your care team**

- Keep the list exactly as your child's care team gives it to you. Don't rewrite it from memory.
- Ask the care team to check it with you when something changes.
- If you're ever unsure about a medicine, how much to give or when, call your care team. Don't guess.

**Questions**

- A page for questions as they come up, to bring to the next visit. See "Questions to ask your care team".

**Notes from visits and calls**

- The date, who you spoke with, what they said and what happens next.

**The urgent-signs list**

- The list your care team gave you of signs that mean you should call right away. See "When to call your care team".

**Papers**

- A pocket or folder for insurance letters, bills, receipts and school or work forms.

## Tips that help

- Bring the notebook to every visit.
- Take photos of important papers as a backup.
- If someone else takes your child to an appointment, give them the notebook.
- Some hospitals have an online patient portal with test dates and messages. Ask the care team how to use it. It doesn't replace your notebook.

## Who to ask

Your child's care team for the medicines list and the urgent-signs list. The hospital social worker if you'd like help getting organized.
