Your child's care team is the right place for every question about your child's health. Visits can feel rushed, so it helps to come with your questions written down. There are no silly questions.

## Before the visit

- Write your questions down as they come up, in a notebook or on your phone.
- Put the most important ones first, in case time runs short.
- If you can, bring another adult to listen and take notes.

## Questions many families ask

**About who to call**

- Who do we call with questions during the day, and after hours?
- What should we do if we can't reach anyone?

**About what to watch for**

- What should we watch for at home, and when should we call you?
- Can you give us that list in writing?

**About the plan**

- What happens next, and when?
- How will we know if the plan changes?
- Is there anything our child shouldn't do right now, like school, sports or being in crowds?

**About daily life**

- Are there any rules about food, visitors or pets at home?
- Who can help us with school, work or money questions?

## During the visit

- It's okay to ask them to slow down, repeat something or use simpler words.
- Repeat back what you heard: "So what you're saying is…". This catches mix-ups.
- Ask for important information in writing.
- If you'd rather talk in Spanish or another language, ask for an interpreter. Most hospitals can arrange one.

## After the visit

- Write down what you heard and what happens next, while it's fresh.
- If you remember a question later, write it down for next time, or call if it can't wait.

## If it can't wait

If you're worried about your child and it can't wait for the next visit, call the number your care team gave you for questions. If you think it's an emergency, call 911.

## Who to ask

Your child's doctors and nurses for anything about health and treatment. The hospital social worker for help with practical questions.
