Caring for your child is a long stretch, not a sprint. Looking after yourself isn't selfish. It's part of how you keep going for your child and your family.

## The basics, on hard days

- **Sleep when you can.** If there are two caregivers, take turns staying overnight. Even one good night helps.
- **Eat and drink.** Keep snacks and a water bottle in your bag. Meals are easy to skip at the hospital.
- **Take short breaks.** A walk outside, a shower, ten quiet minutes. Ask a nurse when it's a good time to step away.
- **Look after your own health.** Keep your own appointments and take your own medicines.

## Accept help, and be specific

People often want to help but don't know how. Make a short list of real tasks:

- driving to an appointment;
- bringing a meal or groceries;
- picking up your other children from school;
- doing laundry or walking the dog;
- sitting with your child so you can rest.

When someone asks "How can I help?", pick one thing from the list. You can ask one friend to coordinate, so you don't have to answer every message.

## Talk to someone

- The hospital social worker can listen, and help you find counseling or support groups for parents.
- Many families find it helps to talk with other parents who understand.
- Your own doctor is a good place to go if you're not sleeping, not eating or feeling low most days.

## When it's too much

It's normal to feel scared, angry, numb or exhausted. If those feelings stay for a long time, or start to feel unbearable, reach out to the hospital social worker or your own doctor.

If you're in emotional crisis, call or text 988 to reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, any time, day or night. Spanish is available.

If you or anyone else is in immediate danger, call 911. Outside the US, contact your local emergency services.

## Who to ask

The hospital social worker for counseling, support groups and practical help. Your own doctor for your health. In a crisis, the crisis line above. In an emergency, 911.
